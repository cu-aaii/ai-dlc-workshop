What is AI-DLC?

AI-DLC (AI-Driven Development Lifecycle) is a methodology AWS developed to rebuild the software development lifecycle around AI from the ground up, rather than bolting AI onto existing Agile or Scrum. It treats AI as a central collaborator on the team. The core loop is AI-powered execution with human oversight: AI drafts a detailed plan, asks clarifying questions, and only implements after a human validates the plan and the result. People stay in control of every meaningful decision.

The vocabulary maps closely onto Agile, so the shift is easy to follow:

  • Sprints become "bolts" — shorter, more intense work cycles measured in hours or days rather than weeks.
  • Epics become "Units of Work" — self-contained chunks a team can build and deploy independently.
  • Planning & backlog refinement become "Mob Elaboration" — the whole team validates AI's questions and proposals together, in real time.

Work moves through three phases, each feeding richer context into the next:

1 · Inception
AI turns business intent into requirements and Units of Work via Mob Elaboration.
2 · Construction
AI proposes architecture, design, code, and tests via "Mob Construction" while the team clarifies decisions live.
3 · Operations
AI manages infrastructure-as-code and deployment under the team's oversight.

This is designed for your whole role — not just engineers

A common misread is that a "build" workshop is only for developers. AI-DLC is the opposite: the methodology only works when the entire cross-functional team is in the room. Mob Elaboration and Mob Construction depend on every perspective being present to validate decisions as they're made — the requirements, the risks, the operational realities, and the business intent, not just the code.

So this is built for everyone who touches the development lifecycle:

Software & platform engineers Project & program managers Product owners IT service management IT security Business & data analysts Designers Business stakeholders

If your first reaction is "I'm not a coder, why me?" — that's exactly why you're invited. The person who understands the requirement, the compliance boundary, or the user's real problem is essential to the loop. You'll be validating and steering AI's work alongside the engineers, not watching from the side.

What we'll actually build

Over the two days we'll build a real MVP for a real Cornell need: the "blueprint" layer that sits between the AI Platform's backend and the campus builders who create agents and AI-powered applications. It's the connective tissue that lets someone in any unit propose a capability the platform doesn't yet offer, get it agreed on centrally, and ship it as a reusable, governed building block — instead of building a parallel one-off.

We'll run it hackathon-style: we bring a set of blueprints we know we need, and teams self-organize to take the ones they find most valuable, breaking each into Units of Work and short bolts and building with AI. The keystone we're aiming at:

★ The vision

A "Cornell Builder" that deploys for you

A builder describes what they want inside their AI assistant ("automate my team's access to backend tools and reporting"); the platform figures out the right blueprint and deploys a working, governed starting point for them — self-service, on AWS, within Cornell's guardrails. Every other blueprint below is a building block this keystone deploys.

The supporting blueprints teams can pick up:

Chatbots (incl. Microsoft Teams-fronted)

Basic and advanced conversational apps, with Teams as the default frontend for internal users.

Event-driven automations

Agents that act on a schedule or in response to a system event, not just a human prompt.

MCP server hosting & SSO-protected dashboards

Standard, reusable patterns for hosting tools and secure web apps.

Document ETL & batch processing

Turning a pile of documents into something usable — extract, transform, and load into a searchable knowledge store, including large batch LLM jobs.

UI automation of legacy/desktop apps

Automating the systems that have no API by having an agent operate the application the way a person would.

The output is twofold: working components for the platform, and a group of people across units who've lived the methodology and can bring it home to their teams.
